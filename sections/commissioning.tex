\section{On-Sky Commissioning Campaign with LSSTCam}
\label{sec:commissioning}

The \gls{LSSTCam} on-sky commissioning campaign began on \svcampaignstartdate, following the achievement of  \gls{LSSTCam}  first photon on sky, and concluded on \svcampaignenddate.
Its primary objective was to commission and validate and characterize  the fully integrated observatory system of the Simonyi Survey Telescope, including the \gls{AOS}, \gls{LSSTCam}, and the automated \gls{DMS}, all operating together as a single integrated observatory at scale.
This was done with a series of focused engineering tests and the execution of a set of \gls{SV} surveys.
 Following a four-week engineering and operations downtime after the conclusion of the \gls{SV} surveys on \svcampaignenddate the observatory was formally handed over from Construction to Operations on \startoperations,, after which the Science Validation surveys resumed during the Early Operations period.
The full \gls{DP2} dataset spans both the commissioning SV campaign and this early operations phase and includes observations acquired between \svcampaignstartdate and the \gls{DP2} cut off date of \dptwoenddate.
The Early Operations period continued until the start of the LSST on \lsststartdate.

\subsection{Simonyi Survey Telescope}
\label{ssec:simonyi}
%
The Simonyi Survey Telescope \citep[][]{2024SPIE13094E..09S}  features a compact three-mirror design incorporating an 8.4-meter primary--tertiary mirror (\gls{M1M3}) fabricated from a single borosilicate monolith, and a 3.5 m secondary mirror (M2).
This compact \gls{configuration} supports a wide 3.5\degree  field of view while enabling exceptional pointing stability, with slew-and-settle times under 5 seconds.

To achieve the scientific goals of the 10-yr \gls{LSST}, the observatory must maintain high image quality across the full field of view \citep[][]{2008arXiv0805.2366I}.
This is accomplished through the \gls{AOS} \citep[][]{2015ApOpt..54.9045X, 2024ApJ...974..108M}, which corrects, between successive exposures, wavefront distortions caused by optical misalignments and mirror surface deformations, primarily driven by gravitational and thermal loads.

The \gls{AOS} comprises open-loop and closed-loop components.
The open-loop component corrects for predictable distortions using look-up tables as a function of telescope elevation and \gls{camera} rotator angle.
The closed-loop component addresses unpredictable or slowly varying aberrations using curvature wavefront sensing \citep[][]{2023aoel.confE..67T} on the eight corner wavefront sensors of the \gls{LSSTCam} focal plane, which acquire pairs of intra- and extra-focal ``donut'' images to measure wavefront errors.
Corrections are applied to the M2 and \gls{camera} hexapods and to the shape of \gls{M1M3} via its 156-actuator support system, driving the system towards the target optical state within two visits of each measurement.
This represents a significant operational advance over the \gls{LSSTComCam} campaign, during which dedicated wavefront sensors were unavailable and wavefront errors were instead estimated by defocusing the telescope $\pm$1.5\,mm on either side of focus \citep[][]{10.71929/rubin/2570308}.

During the \gls{LSSTCam} commissioning campaign, \gls{AOS} operations were interspersed with dedicated engineering periods focused on wavefront sensing and optical state characterisation, enabling iterative improvements across all components of the system throughout the campaign \citep[][]{SITCOMTN-170}.
The bulk of the \gls{DP2} observations were acquired during the commissioning-era \gls{SV} surveys, a period during which the \gls{AOS} was undergoing intensive characterisation and iterative improvement.
During this time the system contribution to the delivered \gls{PSF} \gls{FWHM} still exceeded the design specification \citep[][]{LSE-29, RTN-122, SOTN-004}.
As a consequence, the majority of \gls{DP2} observations were taken with a larger system contribution to the \gls{PSF} size budget than the system is capable of delivering under comparable atmospheric conditions, producing larger \gls{PSF} \glspl{FWHM} and shallower point-source depths.
The residual optical aberrations also manifest as spatially coherent structure in \gls{PSF} \gls{shape} residuals across the focal plane, with implications for weak lensing analyses and \gls{PSF} model validation.
The quantitative impact on delivered image quality is characterised in \S\ref{ssec:image_quality}, and the \gls{AOS} improvement program and current performance are described in \citet[][]{RTN-122} and \citet[][]{SOTN-004}.

\subsection{LSSTCam}
\label{ssec:lsstcam}
%
\gls{LSSTCam} \citep[][]{10.1117/12.3018987, 10.71929/rubin/2571927} is a 3.2-gigapixel mosaic camera with a 9.6 deg$^{\rm 2}$ instantaneous field of view.
Its focal plane comprises 25 modular rafts arranged in a 5$\xspace\times\xspace$5 grid: 21 science rafts, each containing nine 4K$\xspace\times\xspace$4K \gls{CCD} sensors, and 4 corner rafts each housing two wavefront sensors and two guider sensors, for a total of 205 sensors: 189 science, 8 wavefront, and 8 guider.

\gls{LSSTCam} employs sensors from two vendors: \gls{E2V} sensors populate the central science rafts, and \gls{ITL} sensors populate the peripheral science rafts.
Each sensor is subdivided into 16 amplifiers, each reading 512$\xspace\times\xspace$2048 pixels in parallel.
\figref{fig:lsstcam_schematic} shows the \gls{LSSTCam} focal plane layout, viewed from above looking down through the camera lenses, including the placement and numbering scheme of sensors (``S'') and amplifiers (``C''); a full description is given in \citet[][]{2025JATIS..11a1209P, ctn-001}.

\plotone{LSSTCam_focal_plane}
\caption{\gls{LSSTCam} focal plane layout, showing the placement and numbering scheme of sensors (``S'') and amplifiers (``C''). The view is from above, looking through the \gls{LSSTCam} lenses onto the focal plane. Each sensor contains 16 amplifiers. Each raft is composed of a 3$\xspace\times\xspace$3 array of sensors. The detector number for each sensor is indicated in parentheses.}

During the commissioning campaign, only 188 of the 189 science sensors were operational; sensor R30/S12 was non-operational throughout and is excluded from all \gls{DP2} data products.
The eight heavily vignetted corner science sensors (detectors), R01/S00 (0), R03/S02 (20), R10/S00 (27), R14/S02 (65), R30/S20 (123), R34/S22 (161), R41/S20 (168), and R43/S22 (188), are also excluded from all \gls{DP2} processing, even though they were functional.
Science sensors (detectors) R03/S20 (24), R30/S10 (120), R30/S11 (121), and R42/S00 (172) are additionally excluded from data acquired before 2025-07-03 as the \gls{calibration} data required to process them were not yet available.
Any additional exclusions that were applied during the processing stage are described in \secref{sec:drp}.

The median amplifier loses approximately 0.45\% of its area to static defects, with the mean across amplifiers closer to 0.7\%  \citep[][]{SITCOMTN-170}.
Overall, more than 99\% of the active focal-plane area within the calibrated field is usable \citep[][]{SITCOMTN-170}.
Optical vignetting limits the fully calibrated field radius to 317\,mm; a partially vignetted zone extends to 350\,mm, beyond which pixels are excluded from science products.

No new static defect classes were identified on either \gls{ITL} or \gls{E2V} sensors during commissioning beyond those already identified during commissioning with \gls{LSSTComCam} \citep[][]{rtn-095} or in the laboratory \citep[][]{SITCOMTN-148, SITCOMTN-170}.
A subset of \gls{ITL} sensors exhibits ``vampire'' pixel anomalies and locally elevated readout noise consistent with \gls{LSSTComCam} commissioning.
Dynamic masking, including treatment of \gls{E2V} edge bleeds and occasional bright-star flare artifacts at extreme detector edges, is ongoing \citep[][]{SITCOMTN-170}.

\subsubsection{Filter Complement}
\label{sssec:lsstcam_filters}
%
\gls{LSSTCam} supports imaging with the same six broadband $ugrizy$ filters, spanning 320--1050~nm, as \gls{LSSTComCam}, using an identical filter design.
Its filter exchanger can hold five filters at a time, as opposed to only three for \gls{LSSTComCam}.

Unlike the \gls{LSSTComCam} bandpasses, which were built entirely from fiducial mirror, lens, filter, and detector quantum-efficiency curves in the absence of any device-specific measurements, the \gls{DP2} standard bandpasses incorporate real measurements where available.
The fiducial mirror and lens throughputs are still used, since detector- and mirror-level measurements have not yet been incorporated into \gls{DP2}.
The filter throughput, however, is now the focal-plane average of vendor filter scans, using the same underlying filter design as \gls{LSSTComCam} but capturing real variation across the field of view.
The detector quantum efficiency is likewise the focal-plane average over all the \gls{ITL} and \gls{E2V} detectors combined.

\gls{ITL} and \gls{E2V} sensors are not identical: \gls{ITL} devices have somewhat higher quantum efficiency in the red, so the focal-plane-averaged \gls{LSSTCam} response is marginally lower than the all-\gls{ITL} response used for \gls{LSSTComCam}.
This chromatic difference is most apparent in the $g$ band, visible even by eye as a two-tone effect across the focal plane.
$u$-band throughput is dominated by the atmosphere and filter rather than the detector, and the redder bands are comparatively insensitive to the vendor difference.
In absolute terms the \gls{ITL}/\gls{E2V} difference is small, much smaller than, for example, the difference between \gls{LSSTCam} and other instruments such as \gls{DECam}, since the two vendors' filters are essentially the same design.
It is nonetheless large enough to matter for millimagnitude-level photometric \gls{calibration}.

Individual per-detector quantum-efficiency curves have not yet been released.
The standard \gls{passband} distributed with \gls{DP2} is therefore the focal-plane average, consistent with how the survey is calibrated and appropriate for use with the deep coadds.
The full-system throughput of the six \gls{LSSTCam} filters is shown in \figref{fig:lsstcam_standard_bandpasses} and the corresponding transmission curves are provided as a \gls{DP2} data product(\secref{sssec:std_passbands}).

\subsection{Calibration System}
\label{ssec:calibration_system}
%
Unlike the \gls{LSSTComCam} campaign, during which the Rubin calibration system had not yet been installed and flat fielding relied entirely on twilight flats \citep[][]{rtn-095}, the \gls{LSSTCam} commissioning campaign had access to the Rubin flat field illumination system \citep[][]{sitcomtn-086}.
The baseline calibration plan for the as-built system is described in \citet[][]{sitcomtn-086}; a full description of \gls{ISR} procedures and calibration data products is given in \citet[][]{10.1117/1.JATIS.11.1.011209}.

The calibration system comprises a dome flat field screen, a \gls{CBP}, a \gls{NIST}-traceable silicon photodiode electrometer, and the \gls{AuxTel} for atmospheric monitoring.
During the commissioning campaign, the \gls{CBP} was deployed at scale on \gls{LSSTCam} for the first time, providing in-situ measurements of the transmission profiles of five of the six \gls{LSST} broadband filters, $ugrizy$, across multiple primary mirror pupil positions, and characterising the angle-of-incidence-dependent blue-shift of the filter bandpass edges across the focal plane \citep[][]{2026arXiv260631822A}.
Achieving absolute throughput calibration at the millimagnitude level  required by the \gls{LSST} requires a more precise characterisation of the \gls{CBP} transmission and a partial-pupil vignetting model; both are the subject of ongoing work \citep[][]{2026arXiv260631822A}.

Calibration products generated for \gls{DP2} processing include bias frames, dark frames, dome flat fields, \glspl{PTC}, cross talk matrices, brighter-fatter kernels, linearity corrections, and \gls{CTI} models \citep[][]{10.1117/1.JATIS.11.1.011209}.
Dome flat fields were used as the primary flat field source, supplemented by a star-flat-based illumination correction and, where dome illumination was unavailable, twilight flats \citep[][]{10.1117/1.JATIS.11.1.011209}.
Certified calibration products are versioned in the \gls{USDF} archive and transferred to the summit as needed.
The full suite of calibration and throughput data products included in \gls{DP2} is described in \secref{sec:data_products}.

\subsubsection{Small-Field Observations}
\label{sssec:small_fields}
%
Several small fields were observed prior to the start of the \gls{SV} surveys, with some observations overlapping the early portion of the \gls{SV} survey period.
All small-field observations, including those from the First Look campaign, are included in \gls{DP2}.
Observations were typically acquired in sequences of at least 10 visits per bandpass, cycling through the available filters, with field selection guided by commissioning priorities and community input.
Several of these fields, including \gls{COSMOS}, M49, the Prawn Nebula region, and Rubin\_SV\_225\_-40, received substantial numbers of visits and are among the most deeply observed regions in \gls{DP2}.
The \gls{COSMOS} field was first observed as part of the small-field campaign but was not accessible during the main \gls{SV} survey period; additional observations were later acquired during the Early Operations phase.

The \gls{SV} surveys began on \svcampaignstartdate and comprised two interleaved components within a single \gls{FBS} configuration, a wide survey and a deep survey.

The Wide Survey covered an initial footprint of $\sim$\,3,000\,deg$^2$ tracing the ecliptic plane from dense regions of the Galactic Bulge through low-dust regions of the planned \gls{LSST} \gls{WFD} footprint.
Approximately halfway through the SV period, the footprint was consolidated to $\sim$\,750\,deg$^2$, and further to $\sim$\,300\,deg$^2$ in the final week of commissioning to concentrate visits and achieve the depth required for difference-image testing.
As a result, median visit counts and coadded depth increase substantially toward the inner regions of the footprint: the full 3,000\,deg$^2$ region reaches a median of 38 visits across all bands ($m\_5 \approx 25.0$), the 750\,deg$^2$ subset reaches 56 visits ($m\_5 \approx 25.8$), and the 300\,deg$^2$ core reaches 64 visits ($m\_5 \approx 25.7$).

\subsubsection{Early Operations Observations}
\label{sssec:early_ops}
%
Following the transition to Operations on \startoperations, \gls{FBS}-driven observations resumed during the Early Operations optimization period.
Observations during this period, which ran from \startoperations through \dptwoenddate, were a mixture of engineering activities and \gls{FBS}-driven survey observations aimed at improving image quality and survey speed in preparation to  start the \gls{LSST}, and  building difference-imaging templates across the full \gls{LSST} footprint..

The pre-\gls{LSST} observations are characterised by a lower and less regular cadence than
the \gls{SV} surveys, reflecting frequent engineering interruptions.
The \gls{FBS} was configured with a single-visit template tier as a scheduling priority, and on any given night would typically concentrate observations in a single band rather than interleaving filters, to maximise sky coverage for template building.
The $i$ band was preferred  due to its high throughput and suitability for difference imaging.
A concentrated block of $i$-band observations in late October 2025 reflected deliberate \gls{AOS} engineering tests that held the filter fixed to isolate optical state changes from filter-induced perturbations, producing the prominent $i$-band coverage regions visible in
\figref{fig:dp2_sky} \citep[][]{survey-strategy-dp2}.
Pre-\gls{LSST} visits not overlapping existing \gls{SV} survey regions were not combined into coadds but are included in the \gls{DP2} visit dataset.

\subsection{Data Processing}
\label{ssec:data processing}
%
The Rubin \gls{DMS} is responsible for the acquisition, transfer, archiving, processing, and distribution of all data produced by the observatory.
It operates as a fully automated end-to-end system, ingesting raw pixels from the \gls{LSSTCam} focal plane within seconds of shutter close and delivering science-ready data products to a global community of researchers within 120 secs.

The data products in \gls{DP2} are the result of \gls{DRP} (\secref{sec:drp}), which reprocesses the full accumulated image archive to produce the deep, well-calibrated data products that form the basis of each Data Release,  and are delivered to the community via the \gls{RSP} (\secref{sec:data_services}).
During the \gls{LSSTCam} commissioning campaign, the \gls{DMS} was operated at full scale for the first time, transferring, archiving, and processing all \gls{LSSTCam} images and validating the end-to-end data flow from summit to \gls{RSP}.

\subsubsection{Data Acquisition, Transfer and Archive}
\label{ssec:dataacq}
Following each exposure, the \gls{DAQ} system at the summit stores the raw image data as a set of 205 files per exposure: 189 science sensors\footnote{Only 188 science sensor files were generated per exposure  as sensor R30/S12 was non-operational throughout; see \S\ref{ssec:lsstcam}.}, 8 wavefront sensors, and 8 guiding sensors on the \gls{LSSTCam} focal plane.
Uncompressed, a full science exposure totals $\sim$\,8.2\,GB; after compression, $\sim$\,4\,GB.

At the summit, each exposure is processed in near real time by the Rapid Analysis pipeline, performing \gls{ISR} (\secref{ssec:isr}) and source detection (\secref{ssec:detection}) on a 30--60 second timescale to provide quick-look image diagnostics and support real-time \gls{AOS} assessment.
The resulting per-visit quality metrics formed the basis of the visit quality characterisations used to define the \gls{DP2} coadd inputs.
Summary visit and detector-level metadata are available to users as \gls{DP2} data products (\secref{ssec:metadata}) and are also available in an opsim-format SQLite database ()\secref{sssec:opsim}).

\subsubsection{LSST Science Pipelines}
\label{ssec:pipelines}
%
The \gls{DP2} science-ready data products in are the result of dedicated \gls{DRP} campaigns executed at the \gls{USDF} at \gls{SLAC}.
\gls{DP2} processing was done using version \sciencepipelinesversion of the LSST Science Pipelines \citep[][]{PSTN-019} and follows a four-stage structure.
Stage~1 performs single-visit processing, including \gls{ISR}, source detection, astrometric and photometric calibration, and \gls{PSF} characterisation.
Stage~2 performs global recalibration, Gaussian-process modelling of atmospheric turbulence, proper motion and parallax fitting, and \gls{PSF} re-estimation using a standard star set.
Stage~3 performs coaddition and measurement on coadded images, including the generation of the Object catalog.
Stage~4 performs \gls{DIA} and  solar system object processing, and the generation of light curves via forced photometry.
Each stage underwent continuous data quality monitoring and a formal verification and validation review before processing advanced to the subsequent stage.
A full description of the processing pipelines, algorithms, and configuration used for \gls{DP2} is given in \secref{sec:drp}.

\subsection{Delivered Image Quality}
\label{ssec:image_quality}
%
The delivered image quality at Rubin Observatory reflects contributions from the atmosphere, dome environment, telescope optics, \gls{AOS}, and LSSTCam.
The design specification for the median delivered \gls{PSF} \gls{FWHM}, referenced to zenith at 550,nm, is 0\farcs7 \citep[][]{LPM-17}, corresponding to the measured median atmospheric seeing of 0\farcs72 at Cerro Pach'on \citep[][]{SOTN-004} added in quadrature with a total system contribution of 0\farcs35 \citep[][]{SOTN-004}.
During the period covered by the \gls{DP2} dataset, the system had not yet consistently met this design specification \citep[][]{SOTN-004, SITCOMTN-170}.
The primary excess contributions are from the optical system,  including residual \gls{AOS} errors and mirror thermal gradients, as well as from dome seeing.
The current total system contribution to \gls{PSF} \gls{FWHM} is approximately 0\farcs55--0\farcs60, compared to the design allocation of 0\farcs40.
At the median survey airmass of 1.2, an atmospheric contribution of 0\farcs72 at zenith corresponds to $\sim$\,0\farcs80; combined in quadrature with the current system contribution, this is consistent with the observed median delivered image quality of $\sim$\,1\farcs0 \gls{PSF} \gls{FWHM} in the $riz$ bands during the \gls{DP2} observations \citep[][]{RTN-122}.
