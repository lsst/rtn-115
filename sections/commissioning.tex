\section{On-Sky Commissioning Campaign with LSSTCam}
\label{sec:commissioning}

The \gls{LSSTCam} on-sky commissioning campaign began on \svcampaignstartdate, following the achievemnt of  \gls{LSSTCam}  first photon on sky, and concluded on \svcampaignenddate.
Its primary objective was to commission and validate and characterize  the fully integrated observatory system of Simonyi Survey Telescope, including the active optics system (AOS), the  LSST Science Camera (LSSTCam), and the automated \gls{DMS}, all operating together as a single integrated observatory at scale.
This was done with a series of focused engineering tests and the eecution of a set of Science Validation (SV) surveys.
 Following a four-week engineering and operations downtime after the conclusion of the SV surveys on \svcampaignenddate the observatory was formally handed over from Construction to Operationw on \startoperations,, after which the Science Validation surveys resumed during the Early Operations period.
The full \gls{DP2} dataset spans both the commissioning SV campaign and this early operations phase covering observations acquired between \svcampaignstartdate\ and \dptwoenddate.

\subsubsection{Filter Complement}
\label{sssec:lsstcam_filters}

\gls{LSSTCam} supports imaging with the same six broadband filters $ugrizy$, spanning 320--1050~nm, as \gls{LSSTComCam}, using an identical filter design.
Its filter exchanger can hold five filters at a time, as opposed to only three for \gls{LSSTComCam}.

Unlike the \gls{LSSTComCam} bandpasses, which were built entirely from fiducial mirror, lens, filter, and \gls{ITL} detector quantum-efficiency curves in the absence of any device-specific measurements, the \gls{DP2} standard bandpasses incorporate real measurements where available.
The fiducial mirror and lens throughputs are still used, since detector- and mirror-level measurements have not yet been incorporated into \gls{DP2}.
The filter throughput, however, is now the focal-plane average of vendor filter scans, using the same underlying filter design as \gls{LSSTComCam} but now capturing real variation across the field of view.
The detector quantum efficiency is likewise the focal-plane average \gls{ITL} and \gls{E2V}.

\gls{ITL} and \gls{E2V} sensors are not identical: \gls{ITL} devices have somewhat higher quantum efficiency in the red, so the focal-plane-averaged \gls{LSSTCam} response is marginally lower than the all-\gls{ITL} response used for \gls{LSSTComCam}.
This chromatic difference is most apparent in the $g$ band, visible even by eye as a two-tone effect across the focal plane, while $u$-band throughput is dominated by the atmosphere and filter rather than the detector, and the redder bands are comparatively insensitive to the vendor difference.
In absolute terms the \gls{ITL}/\gls{E2V} difference is small, much smaller than, for example, the difference between \gls{LSSTCam} and other instruments such as DECam, since the two vendors' filters are essentially the same design.
It is nonetheless large enough to matter for millimagnitude-level photometric calibration.

Individual per-detector quantum-efficiency curves have not yet been released. The standard passband distributed with \gls{DP2} is therefore the focal-plane average, consistent with how the survey is calibrated and appropriate for use with the deep coadds.
The full-system throughput of the six \gls{LSSTCam} filters is shown in \figref{fig:lsstcam_standard_bandpasses} and the corresponding transmission curves are provided as a \gls{DP2} data product(\secref{sssec:std_passbands}}).

\item For LSSTCam we do have measurements but they aren’t incorporated into DP2 so we use the fiducial mirrors/lenses but focal plane average of the vendor filter scans (which is the same as the fiducial filters for ComCam but the calibration knows about the variations over the FOV), and then the focal plane average of ITL and E2V
