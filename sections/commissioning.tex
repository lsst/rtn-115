\section{On-Sky Commissioning Campaign with LSSTCam}
\label{sec:commissioning}

The \gls{LSSTCam} on-sky commissioning campaign began on \svcampaignstartdate, following the achievemnt of  \gls{LSSTCam}  first photon on sky, and concluded on \svcampaignenddate.
Its primary objective was to commission and validate and characterize  the fully integrated observatory system of Simonyi Survey Telescope, including the active optics system (AOS), the  LSST Science Camera (LSSTCam), and the automated \gls{DMS}, all operating together as a single integrated observatory at scale.
This was done with a series of focused engineering tests and the eecution of a set of Science Validation (SV) surveys.
 Following a four-week engineering and operations downtime after the conclusion of the SV surveys on \svcampaignenddate the observatory was formally handed over from Construction to Operationw on \startoperations,, after which the Science Validation surveys resumed during the Early Operations period.
The full \gls{DP2} dataset spans both the commissioning SV campaign and this early operations phase covering observations acquired between \svcampaignstartdate\ and \dptwoenddate.

\subsubsection{Filter Complement}
\label{sssec:lsstcam_filters}

\gls{LSSTCam} supports imaging with the same six broadband filters $ugrizy$, spanning 320--1050~nm, as \gls{LSSTComCam}, using an identical filter design.
Its filter exchanger can hold five filters at a time, as opposed to only three for \gls{LSSTComCam}.

Unlike the \gls{LSSTComCam} bandpasses, which were built entirely from fiducial mirror, lens, filter, and detector quantum-efficiency curves in the absence of any device-specific measurements, the \gls{DP2} standard bandpasses incorporate real measurements where available.
The fiducial mirror and lens throughputs are still used, since detector- and mirror-level measurements have not yet been incorporated into \gls{DP2}.
The filter throughput, however, is now the focal-plane average of vendor filter scans, using the same underlying filter design as \gls{LSSTComCam} but now capturing real variation across the field of view.
The detector quantum efficiency is likewise the focal-plane average \gls{ITL} and \gls{E2V}.

\gls{ITL} and \gls{E2V} sensors are not identical: \gls{ITL} devices have somewhat higher quantum efficiency in the red, so the focal-plane-averaged \gls{LSSTCam} response is marginally lower than the all-\gls{ITL} response used for \gls{LSSTComCam}.
This chromatic difference is most apparent in the $g$ band, visible even by eye as a two-tone effect across the focal plane.
$u$-band throughput is dominated by the atmosphere and filter rather than the detector, and the redder bands are comparatively insensitive to the vendor difference.
In absolute terms the \gls{ITL}/\gls{E2V} difference is small, much smaller than, for example, the difference between \gls{LSSTCam} and other instruments such as DECam, since the two vendors' filters are essentially the same design.
It is nonetheless large enough to matter for millimagnitude-level photometric calibration.

Individual per-detector quantum-efficiency curves have not yet been released.
The standard passband distributed with \gls{DP2} is therefore the focal-plane average, consistent with how the survey is calibrated and appropriate for use with the deep coadds.

\subsection{Calibration System}
\label{ssec:calibration_system}
%
Unlike the \gls{LSSTComCam} campaign, during which the Rubin calibration system had not yet been installed and flat fielding relied entirely on twilight flats \citep[][]{rtn-095}, the \gls{LSSTCam} commissioning campaign had access to the Rubin flat field illumination system \citep[][]{sitcomtn-086}.
The baseline calibration plan for the as-built system is described in \citet{sitcomtn-086}; a full description of \gls{ISR} procedures and calibration data products is given in \citet{10.1117/1.JATIS.11.1.011209}.

The calibration system comprises a dome flat field screen, a \gls{CBP}, a NIST-traceable silicon photodiode electrometer, and the \gls{AuxTel} for atmospheric monitoring.
During the commissioning campaign, the \gls{CBP} was deployed at scale on \gls{LSSTCam} for the first time, providing in-situ measurements of the transmission profiles of five of the six \gls{LSST} broadband filters, $ugrizy$ across multiple primary mirror pupil positions, and characterising the angle-of-incidence-dependent blue-shift of the filter bandpass edges across the focal plane \citep[][]{2606.31822}.
Achieving absolute throughput calibration at the millimagnitude level  required by the \gls{LSST} requires a more precise characterisation of the \gls{CBP} transmission and a partial-pupil vignetting model; both are the subject of ongoing work \citep[][]{2606.31822}.

Calibration products generated for \gls{DP2} processing include bias frames, dark frames, dome flat fields, \glspl{PTC}, crosstalk matrices, brighter-fatter kernels, linearity corrections, and \gls{CTI} models \citep[][]{plazas2025}.
Dome flat fields were used as the primary flat field source, supplemented by a star-flat-based illumination correction and, where dome illumination was unavailable, twilight flats \citep[][]{plazas2025}.
Certified calibration products are versioned in the \gls{USDF} archive and transferred to the summit as needed.
The full suite of calibration and throughput data products included in \gls{DP2} is described in \secref{sec:data_products}.
