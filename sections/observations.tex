\section{Science Program Observations}
\label{sec:science_program}
%
The science program observations that form the basis of \gls{DP2} were acquired in three phases during Rubin commissioning and early operations.
The first phase comprised small-field observations taken shortly after the \gls{LSSTCam} First Photon milestone to support the Rubin First Look event (\secref{ssec:small_fields}).
The second phase comprised the commissioning-era \gls{SV} survey campaigns, which ran from \svcampaignstartdate to \svcampaignenddate (\secref{ssec:sv_surveys}).
A four-week engineering downtime followed the \gls{SV} campaigns, after which the observatory was formally handed over from the Construction project to Rubin \gls{Operations} on \startoperations.
The subsequent third and final phase comprised pre-\gls{LSST} Early \gls{Operations} observations through the \gls{DP2} cutoff date of \dptwoenddate (\secref{ssec:early_ops}).

All science program observations were scheduled by the \gls{FBS}, which selects visits adaptively to balance sky coverage, filter distribution, airmass, and lunar phase constraints \citep[][]{2026SPIE14151E..0XJ}.
Because the system was still being commissioned and optimized, engineering and commissioning priorities rather than a scientifically optimized observing strategy drove visit acquisition.
Engineering observations are not scientifically useful and are not included in \gls{DP2}.

\figref{fig:dp2_sky} shows the sky distribution of \gls{DP2} visits, the successive \gls{SV} Wide Survey footprints, the coadded \gls{tract} coverage, and the locations of the small fields and \glspl{DDF}.
Coadded images cover approximately \coaddedarea, concentrated in the \gls{SV} Wide \gls{footprint} along the ecliptic, the small fields, and the \glspl{DDF}.

The following sections describe the small fields, the wide-area \gls{SV} survey, and the Deep Drilling Fields.
For each field we report the sky coverage  and temporal distribution of the visits, and the depth of the deep coadds.
We express depth as $m_5$, the $5\sigma$ limiting magnitude for point-like sources \citep[][]{SMTN-002, DMTN-296}, taken from the survey property map (\secref{sssec:propertymaps}) of the PSF magnitude limit.
For each field and band we report the median of $m_5$ over the map pixels within the field.

% Small fields
\subsection{Small-Field Observations}
\label{ssec:small_fields}
%
Several small fields were observed between 2025-04-2525 and 2025-07-29, prior to and during the early part of the \gls{SV} survey period; none were observed during the Early \gls{Operations} phase.
All small-field observations, including those from the First Look campaign, are included in \gls{DP2}.
Observations were typically acquired in sequences of at least 10 visits per bandpass, cycling through the available filters, with field selection guided by commissioning priorities and community input.
Several of these fields, including  M49, and the Prawn Nebula region, received substantial numbers of visits and are among the most deeply observed regions in \gls{DP2}.
\tabref{tab:dp2_sf_visits} lists the central coordinates, per-band visit counts, number of distinct observing nights, and temporal baseline for each small field.
The field center is the mid-range of the boresight R.A.\ and decl.\ across all associated \gls{DP2} visits, as identified by the scheduler.

\input{tables/dp2_sf_visits}

Most small fields were observed in short, concentrated campaigns rather than over extended time periods.
Rubin\_SV\_225\_-40 is the only small field with sustained sampling, with observations on 27 distinct nights over 90 days in all six bands.
New\_Horizons and the Prawn Nebula each received a block of consecutive nights followed by one or two isolated revisits one to two months later, so their baselines (63 and 80 days) overstate the continuity of their coverage.
Abell\_2764, \gls{DESI}\_SV3\_R1 and Rubin\_SV\_280\_-48 were observed on a single night only, and Rubin\_SV\_216\_-17, Rubin\_SV\_300\_-41 and Rubin\_SV\_320\_-15 on two or three nights within a single one-week period.
 The small-field data are therefore suited to studies on timescales of days to weeks, but provide little constraint on variability over months, except for Rubin\_SV\_225\_-40.

The filter coverage reflects the \gls{configuration} of the \gls{LSSTCam} filter carousel, which holds five of the six filters at any time.
The $z$ and $y$ filters were first loaded in mid-May 2025, so fields observed in the first two weeks have no $z$- or $y$-band data; $z$-band coverage in the small fields begins in late May and $y$-band in early June.
Thereafter the $u$ and $y$ filters were exchanged with the lunar phase, so $u$-band observations cluster around new moon and $y$-band observations around full moon.
A fault in the filter exchange system on 2025-07-24 left only $griz$ available for the rest of the \gls{SV} campaign.
The small-field data are therefore suited to studies on timescales of days to weeks, but provide little constraint on variability over months, except for Rubin\_SV\_225\_-40.

\subsection{Science Validation Surveys}
\label{ssec:sv_surveys}
%
The \gls{SV} surveys began on \svcampaignstartdate and comprised two interleaved components within a single \gls{FBS} \gls{configuration}, a wide survey and a deep survey.

\subsubsection{The Wide Survey}
The SV Wide Survey initially targeted a $\sim$\,3,000\,deg$^2$ \gls{footprint} tracing the ecliptic plane from the dense regions of the Galactic Bulge through low-dust regions of the planned \gls{LSST} \gls{WFD} \gls{footprint} (navy outline, \figref{fig:dp2_sky}).
Approximately halfway through the \gls{SV} period, on \svwfdsevenfiftystart, the \gls{footprint} was consolidated to $\sim$\,750\,deg$^2$ (blue outline, \figref{fig:dp2_sky}) and observed in the $griz$ bands only.
This reduced \gls{footprint} is a $10\degr$-wide band centered on the ecliptic, defined by ecliptic longitude $285\degr < \lambda < 360\degr$ and ecliptic latitude $|\beta| < 5\degr$.
On \svwfdthreehundredstart, in the final week of commissioning, the \gls{footprint} was further reduced to the $\sim$\,300\,deg$^2$ area (light-blue outline, \figref{fig:dp2_sky}) covered by newly deployed templates ($300\degr <$ R.A. $< 324\degr$, $-26\degr <$ decl. $< -10\degr$, and $\beta < 5\degr$), to concentrate visits and reach the depth required for difference imaging tests.
As a result, median visit counts and coadded depth increase substantially toward the inner regions of the \gls{footprint}, as visible in the tract density shown in \figref{fig:dp2_sky}.

%%%% Deep survey
\subsubsection{The Deep Survey}
During the \gls{SV} campaign, the Deep Survey targeted ELAIS-S1, \gls{ECDFS}, and \gls{EDFS} (fields A and \gls{B}).
The XMM-\gls{LSS} was observed in $u$ band only.
\gls{COSMOS} was not accessible during the \gls{SV} period, but had been observed during the Small Field campaign before the \gls{SV} campaign and later received additional visits during Early \gls{Operations}.

The \gls{FBS} interleaved \gls{DDF} sequences with the wide survey.
ELAIS-S1 received the largest number of visits and is the only \gls{DDF} observed in all six $ugrizy$ bands.
The remaining \glspl{DDF} were observed primarily in $griz$; following the hardware fault with the filter exchanger on 2025-07-24, $u$- and $y$-band observations were reduced, with the last \gls{DDF} $u$-band visits on 2025-08-10.
The dither pattern for the \glspl{DDF} evolved during the campaign; early observations used only small translational dithers with no intra-night rotation, while later observations introduced both translational and rotational intra-night dithers.

The field center is the mean boresight R.A.\ and decl.\ across all associated \gls{DP2} visits, as identified by the scheduler.

\figref{fig:dp2_ddf_temporal_distribution} shows the temporal sampling of the \glspl{DDF}.
\gls{DDF} observations fall into two blocks separated by the engineering downtime that followed the \gls{SV} campaign: the first, during the \gls{SV} surveys, from late June 2025 to \svcampaignenddate, and the second, during Early \gls{Operations}, from \startoperations\ to \dptwoenddate.
\gls{COSMOS} is the exception: it was observed in the second half of May 2025, before setting for the season, and again from mid-December 2025.
During the \gls{SV} surveys, ELAISS1 was observed from late June, and \gls{ECDFS} and the two \gls{EDFS} fields from mid-July, in runs of several consecutive nights separated by gaps of a few days to two weeks; \gls{ECDFS} and the two \gls{EDFS} fields were observed on nearly the same nights.
XMM-\gls{LSS} was observed during the \gls{SV} surveys only in $u$, on three nights in late July.
All six fields were observed in Early \gls{Operations}, in all six bands.
As for the small fields, $u$-band observations cluster around new moon (late July, mid-November and mid-December) and $y$-band observations around full moon (early July, early November and early January), following the exchange of the $u$ and $y$ filters with the lunar phase.
No \gls{DDF} has continuous coverage over its full baseline, and for \gls{COSMOS} and XMM-\gls{LSS}, whose observations come from two windows several months apart, the baseline in \tabref{tab:dp2_ddf_visits} greatly overstates the continuity of the sampling.

\subsection{Early Operations Observations}
\label{ssec:early_ops}
%
\begin{figure*}[hbt!]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{dp2_nvisits_band}
\caption{Sky distribution of \gls{DP2} visits per band across all three components of the observing program (small-field observations, \gls{SV} surveys, and pre-\gls{LSST} Early Operations observations). Pre-\gls{LSST} Early Operations observations are unevenly distributed across bands, and are mostly located in the large sparsely-covered region to the left of the $\sim$\,3,000\,\gls{deg}$^2$ region that follows the ecliptic.}
\label{fig:dp2_perband_sky}
\end{figure*}

Following the transition to \gls{Operations} on \startoperations, \gls{FBS}-driven observations resumed during the Early \gls{Operations} optimization period.
Observations during this period, which ran from \startoperations through \dptwoenddate, were a mixture of engineering activities and \gls{FBS}-driven survey observations aimed at improving image quality and survey speed in preparation for the start of the \gls{LSST}, and building difference-imaging templates across the full \gls{LSST} \gls{footprint}.
Whereas the \gls{SV} surveys operated at a higher \gls{cadence} than the standard \gls{LSST} survey rate, compressing approximately one year of planned survey visits into roughly three months of on-sky time over a smaller area, the pre-\gls{LSST} observations fell below the \gls{LSST} standard \gls{cadence} due to frequent engineering interruptions.

In addition to the baseline-survey observations, the \gls{FBS} was configured with a single-visit template tier set as the highest scheduling priority, following the recommendation to maximize template sky coverage during early operations \citep[][]{DMTN-107, PSTN-056}.
Since few sky areas had accumulated enough visits to build templates, the \gls{FBS} preferentially scheduled template visits across large areas of sky on any given night where image quality met requirements.
On nights with stable \gls{seeing}, this further tended to concentrate observations in fewer bandpasses rather than interleaving filters, producing an unequal distribution of visits across bands while maximizing sky coverage per night.
The disparity in coverage between the six bands can be seen in \figref{fig:dp2_perband_sky}, which shows the per-band sky distribution of \gls{DP2} visits across the entirety of the DP2 dataset.

Most pre-\gls{LSST} Early \gls{Operations} visits lie in the large, sparsely covered region outside the \gls{SV} Wide footprints (\figref{fig:dp2_sky}).
Visits outside the coadded regions are included in the \gls{DP2} visit dataset but were not combined into coadds.

\subsection{Delivered Image Quality}
\label{ssec:image_quality}
%
The delivered image quality at Rubin Observatory reflects contributions from the atmosphere, dome environment, telescope optics, \gls{AOS}, and \gls{LSSTCam}.
The design specification for the median delivered \gls{PSF} \gls{FWHM}, referenced to zenith at 550\,nm, is 0\farcs7 \citep[][]{LPM-17}, corresponding to the measured median atmospheric seeing of 0\farcs72 at Cerro Pach\'on \citep[][]{SOTN-004} added in quadrature with a total system contribution of 0\farcs35 \citep[][]{SOTN-004}.
During the period covered by the \gls{DP2} dataset, the system had not yet consistently met this design specification \citep[][]{SOTN-004, SITCOMTN-170}.
The primary excess contributions are from the optical system, including residual \gls{AOS} errors and mirror thermal gradients, as well as from dome \gls{seeing}.
The current total system contribution to \gls{PSF} \gls{FWHM} is approximately 0\farcs55--0\farcs60, compared to the design allocation of 0\farcs40.
At the median survey airmass of 1.2, an atmospheric contribution of 0\farcs72 at zenith corresponds to $\sim$\,0\farcs80; combined in quadrature with the current system contribution, this is consistent with the observed median delivered image quality of $\sim$\,1\farcs0 \gls{PSF} \gls{FWHM} in the $riz$ bands during the \gls{DP2} observations \citep[][]{RTN-122}.
