For information regarding the performance and known issues of the data contained in the Early DP2 release, we refer to the Data Quality, Image Artifacts, and Known Issues sections of the Release Documentation at \url{https://dp2.lsst.io}.
A detailed analysis of the validation of DP2 will be provided at the time of its release (expected in the \dptworeleasedate range).

\subsection{Solar System Performance}\label{sec:performance-solarsystem}
There are 299,343 ssObjects (i.e. objects measured at least once by Rubin) in DP2, representing almost 20\% of the known solar system.
136,608 objects are observed at least once in each `griz` band, but it is important to note that the non-simultaneous observations with different filters adds scatter from an object's light curve into the measurement of individual objects' colors.
This can eventually be overcome by per-object light curve fitting, but most objects in DP2 have unreliable colors, and the per-band HErr measurements under-predict objects' unmodeled variability, as visible in the substantial tail of per-band Chi2/dof distribution.

There are 8.1 million diaSources associated to solar system objects in DP2, representing about 2\% of all MPC measurements.
There is a persistent $\sim30$mas bias between DP2 astrometric measurements and ephemeris positions of MPC orbits.
When Rubin astrometry was compared instead to orbits fit only to Gaia astrometry, the $\sim30$ mas bias disappeared.
After substantial investigation and discussion with both the MPC and JPL, we concluded that the bias is $\textbf{not}$ in Rubin astrometry (to $\le$~1mas), but in the orbit catalog. While $30$ mas is small, the median astrometric uncertainty on a DP2 diaSource associated with an ssSource is just 56 mas, making $30$ mas a substantial offset in the context of Rubin precision.
