\subsection{Deblending}
As mentioned in \secref{sec:drp-multivisit-detection}, a new feature of DP2 processing is the introduction of a partial deconvolution step prior to deblending.
Including this step significantly decreases the number of blends.
As a result, in extragalactic fields such as COSMOS, just over half of the objects are not blended in the partially deconvolved image, allowing the deblender to quickly and efficiently recover the flux for objects isolated in observed image and partially deconvolved image.
Most of the remaining blends are small, with only a handful of objects, which scarlet lite deblends fairly easily (see \figref{fig:sample_blend}).
Approximately 10\% of objects come from blends with more than 5 children.

The biggest failure modes are resolved grand-design spirals, which are shredded into multiple poorly modeled objects, and undetected objects in the wings of brighter objects.
The deblender's primary limitation is its memory usage, which in crowded fields can become large, and for DP2 forced us to skip deblending near the galactic plane and a few other dense regions.
We are currently working on algorithms to allow us to process crowded fields and expect most fields to be successfully processed in future releases.
For users wanting a clean sample of objects that are not blended in the partially deconvolved image, meaning objects that are either fully isolated or that overlap neighbors only in the observed image, we recommend selecting objects with \texttt{deblend\_blendNChild} equal to zero or with \texttt{detect\_isIsolated} set.
